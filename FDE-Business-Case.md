## 1. The thesis

The CMO role is bifurcating. One path: the brand-and-demand generalist who commissions technology. The other, emerging and scarce: **the executive who can personally architect the agentic marketing system, prove it works, and scale a team to run it across the org** — while still speaking the language of the CFO, the CRO, and the board.

Scott Stern is deliberately constructing the second profile. Most marketing leaders cannot build. Most builders cannot lead a global org, create a category, or model their own ROI to a CFO. The intersection is the future-ready CMO, and the field is nearly empty.

## 3. The gap in the CMO market

- Most CMOs **commission** AI; they cannot evaluate, build, or harden it. They are negotiating from ignorance.
- Most technical builders cannot **lead** — no category-creation, no IPO-scale org-building, no C-suite peer credibility, no narrative.
- Almost no one holds **both plus finance fluency plus a track record of scaling teams.** That person is hard to find and harder to replace.

## 4. Scott's unfair advantage (the four-way intersection)

1. **Fingers on the keyboard** — builds the system, doesn't just approve it. *(Proof: this program.)*
2. **Knows how to set up the systems** — and how to transfer them to a team. *(Proof: the `fde-delivery-playbook` + artifacts.)*
3. **Technically superior in marketing** — 20+ years enterprise SaaS depth across PMM, demand, AR, adoption, GTM.
4. **Finance + IPO-scale growth + category narrative** — ~$5B IPO contributor, 8→80 team builder, three category creations, speaks CFO/CRO as a peer.

The first two are being built and published now. The combination is not assemblable by a hiring committee from two separate people.
